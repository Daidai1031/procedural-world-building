Uniform random scattering gives every candidate the same chance of being accepted. A **noise map** changes that chance from place to place. Nearby locations have related noise values, so dense areas join into patches instead of appearing as unrelated decisions.

Here the noise value is normalised to the range 0–1. Multiplying it by **density** gives a **placement probability**. At a candidate with probability 0.8, a fresh random value below 0.8 accepts the object. The noise sets the spatial pattern; the random test decides each individual placement inside it.

Lower noise frequency makes larger patches. Higher density accepts more candidates without moving the patches themselves. Drawn as a map next to the placed objects, the patches and the clusters line up.

The two jobs stay separate: frequency controls how big the pattern is, and probability controls how much of it gets filled.
