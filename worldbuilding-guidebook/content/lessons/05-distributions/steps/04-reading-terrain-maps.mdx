Terrain data can describe more than height. A moisture map, a material map, or a distance-to-water function can describe the conditions at each candidate position. The scattering system reads those conditions before deciding whether to place an object.

In this designed world, reeds belong near water but not below its surface. The rule reads two values at each candidate, its **distance to water** and its **height**, and accepts it only when it is close enough to the shore *and* above the water level. Both conditions must hold. A distance band on its own would let "near water" quietly mean "inside the water".

A map can be a stored grid or a function asked for a value at a position. A noise-based moisture map is a useful artistic control, but it is not a physical simulation of moisture, and it should not be presented as one.

The vegetation rules in this lesson are rules of a designed world, not claims about real plant ecology.
