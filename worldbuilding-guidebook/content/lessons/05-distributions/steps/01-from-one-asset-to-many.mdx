An **asset** is a reusable object, such as a rock or a tree. An **instance** is one appearance of that asset in the scene, with its own position, rotation, and scale.

Scattering starts with **candidate positions**. On a height-field terrain, each candidate gets an x and a z coordinate, and a terrain query supplies its y coordinate. A dot can mark the candidate; placing a rock there turns a position into a visible object. The model's base has to sit on that point, or the rock floats or sinks.

Asking for more candidates tests more places. It does not change the size of any rock: how many positions there are and how large each object is are two separate settings. With no filters, every candidate on the terrain is accepted.

Candidates spread evenly over the x–z footprint are not spread evenly over the ground itself: a steep slope covers more surface than its footprint shows, so it receives fewer objects per square metre of ground.

*The interactive scene and practice for this step are planned but not built yet.*
