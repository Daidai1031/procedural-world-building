A population brings at least three asset types together. For each layer, state four things: where its candidates come from, which conditions accept them, how its instances are oriented, and what changes their appearance. A useful protocol explains *why* an object appears at a location, not just how many copies exist.

Every layer reads the same terrain query but keeps its own settings. If crowded objects overlap, add a **minimum spacing** check to that layer. Spacing is one more acceptance rule: it can lower the accepted count even where the terrain is suitable.

Record the seed and parameters of any layout worth coming back to. Compare two populations on the same terrain, then change the terrain and watch how the rules respond. The terrain changes; the protocol stays explicit.

This step gathers the lesson's assignment: trees, bushes, and rocks with independent controls, a repeatable layout, at least one terrain-based acceptance rule, and at least one property driven by terrain data.
