Orientation decides how an object faces after it is placed. A **slope filter** decides whether it may appear at all. A tree can stay upright and still be kept off a cliff.

Normalise the surface normal and the local up direction. Their **dot product** is the cosine of the angle between them: close to 1 on flat ground, smaller on steeper ground. A ground patch is eligible when `dot(normal, up) >= cos(maxSlope)`. Convert degrees to radians before taking the cosine.

Each asset keeps its own limit. A rock can rest on a steep hillside, a tree needs it gentler, a field needs it nearly flat, and an **antenna**, a tall mast that needs a level footing, needs it flatter still. In this designed world the limits are 60, 33, 8, and 5 degrees, giving thresholds of about 0.5, 0.839, 0.990, and 0.996. They are tunable design rules, not ecological or engineering ones.

Lower a limit and more candidates are rejected; raise it and steeper ground opens up. Once a filter is active, the **accepted count** can fall below the **candidate count**, so both numbers are worth showing.

On a spherical planet, local up points outward from the planet centre instead of always along world y.
