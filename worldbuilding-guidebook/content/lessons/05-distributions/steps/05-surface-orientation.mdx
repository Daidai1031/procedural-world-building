title: Surface orientation
goal: Choose an object's orientation independently of its placement position, one rule per asset.

A **surface normal** points straight out of a small patch of ground. It gives an object a direction to line up with: aligning the object's own up direction with the normal makes it tilt with the slope.

Three assets, three rules. A rock looks grounded when it follows the slope. A farmland tile also follows it, lying flat against the ground instead of floating level above a hillside. A tree usually looks more believable standing upright. All three can use the same placement point and still face differently.

**Random yaw** adds variety by turning an object around whichever up direction it uses. Variety does not need random tipping in every direction.

Position, tilt, and yaw are three separate parts of an object's transform. Changing the orientation rule changes how accepted objects face; it does not remove any candidate positions. On flat ground, upright and surface-aligned look identical, so the difference only shows on a slope.
