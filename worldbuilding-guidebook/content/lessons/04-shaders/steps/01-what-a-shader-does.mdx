This terrain is really a tight mesh of tiny triangles (turn on **Wireframe** below to see them). Each triangle corner is a **vertex** — the GPU has to work out where every corner lands on your screen before it can draw anything. That placement job is the **vertex shader**.

Once the triangles are placed, the GPU fills each one in, point by point. Every one of those points is a **fragment**, and the **fragment shader** decides its colour — like colouring inside pencilled lines. Select **Standard material** and **Flat colour shader** and compare: the triangles and camera never change, only this colouring rule does.

Neither shader remembers anything from one frame to the next — every vertex and fragment is worked out fresh, every time, from only what it is handed. That is why a shader can only show a number — height, water, colour — once that number actually reaches it; it cannot go looking for changes on its own.
