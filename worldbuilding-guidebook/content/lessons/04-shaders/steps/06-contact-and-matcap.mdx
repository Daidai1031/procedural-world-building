A **MatCap** photographs a shiny sphere once, then colours every point by only which way it faces the camera — no light needed. The small picture beside **The "photo"** in the legend is that photo, drawn on a canvas rather than loaded from a file.

Toggle **Standard material** and **MatCap**, then orbit the camera: the standard highlight stays fixed to the real light, while MatCap's highlight swims across the surface — read from the photo by angle, not computed from any light.

That angle is a **view-space normal**, the same camera-relative space Step 05 used. It runs -1 to 1, but the photo's coordinates only run 0 to 1 — the highlighted line stretches and shifts one range onto the other. Drag **Highlight angle** to move the bright spot on the photo.

A **contact cue** is a different problem: the small shadow gathering exactly where two surfaces touch, like a fingertip on a table. It needs to know nearby geometry, not just a facing angle — something neither MatCap's one photo nor a standard light can tell you. Only MatCap runs here; contact shadows stay a named idea.
