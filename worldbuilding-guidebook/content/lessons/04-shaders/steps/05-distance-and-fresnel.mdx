A distance-driven shader can highlight an area around a probe, player, or planned excavation. Pass a world-space center as a uniform and measure from the fragment's world position:

```glsl
float d = distance(vWorldPosition, uProbePosition);
float nearProbe = 1.0 - smoothstep(uInnerRadius, uOuterRadius, d);
vec3 color = mix(uBaseColor, uSignalColor, nearProbe);
```

This is a **visual proximity cue**. It does not find buried material unless a separate resource field supplies that information. For a diggable planet, it could distinguish “within tool reach” from “resource confirmed by scanning” with separate data and legends.

Fresnel-style rim intensity responds to the angle between the surface normal and direction toward the camera:

```glsl
float facing = clamp(dot(normalize(vWorldNormal),
                         normalize(uCameraPosition - vWorldPosition)), 0.0, 1.0);
float rim = pow(1.0 - facing, uRimPower);
```

A grazing angle gives a stronger rim. It can outline a selectable voxel chunk, shield, or water edge. The camera position, surface position, and normal must all be in **the same coordinate space**. This expression is a stylized rim mask; a physically based Fresnel reflectance model also needs material reflectance and lighting considerations.

**Compare:** rotate the camera around a fixed rock. The distance-to-probe mask stays put in the world; the Fresnel rim moves with the view.
