Drop a stone in still water and ripples fade out from where it landed — that is what **Distance** does here: a glow that fades around a fixed point, the small marker floating above the ground. It says "near this spot," nothing more; a real prospecting tool would still need actual resource data underneath it, not just proximity.

**Fresnel** works differently. Look straight into a pond and you see through the water; look along its surface at a shallow angle and you mostly see a bright reflection instead. That grazing-angle brightening is what the rim mask reproduces, using the camera's own position rather than any fixed point.

Toggle **Distance** and **Fresnel**, then orbit the camera: the glow stays anchored to its marker; the rim slides around the silhouette instead, because it depends on how you are looking, not where anything is.
