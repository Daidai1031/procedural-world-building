Far away, small detail cannot be seen, so building it is wasted work. **Level of detail** gives each column of chunks a level by its distance from your camera (the highlighted line). Near chunks use every sample, the next ring every second sample, and the farthest every fourth. Orbit and zoom, and the levels follow you. Each step down needs about a quarter of the triangles of the one before.

The catch is at the seams. A coarse chunk and a fine chunk end at slightly different heights along the edge they share, so a slit opens between them. Switch to Left open and look at a seam from the side.

The fix here is to **snap** the finer chunk to the coarser one: every point on its edge slides onto the coarser chunk's edge and borrows its normal there. That closes the height difference from up to 0.36 world units to about 0.01, under a tenth of a finest cell. The two meshes still do not share vertices, so a hairline can remain. Stitching them properly, as the Transvoxel method does, is more work and is not built here.
