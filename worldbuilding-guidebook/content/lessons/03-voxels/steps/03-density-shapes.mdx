A voxel grid only stores numbers, so the formula that produces those numbers decides what world you get. Every shape here is a different formula for the same `sdf(p)` value.

Choose a shape in the control panel. **Ground** is the height-field rule from Step 01, `y - height`. **Caves** starts from that ground, then uses 3D noise to push the value above zero in scattered pockets, which removes the solid there. **Floating islands** drops the ground completely: the value falls below zero only where the noise is high near a middle altitude, so separate masses hang in the air.

The 3D noise is the layered noise from Lesson 2 with a third coordinate. A height field looks it up once per map position. A density field looks it up at every `(x, y, z)`, so it can vary in all three directions, and that is what makes hollows and overhangs possible.

The highlighted lines are the whole cave recipe: a chamber value from noise, and `max` to subtract it from the ground.
