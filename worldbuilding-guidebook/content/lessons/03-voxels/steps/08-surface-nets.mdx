Marching Cubes is one way to mesh a volume, not the only one. **Surface Nets** turns the recipe around. Instead of a point on every crossed edge, it puts one point inside every cell the surface passes through, at the average of that cell's crossing points (the highlighted lines). Then, for every crossed edge, it joins the four cells around that edge into a **quad**.

Switch between the two and turn on Wireframe. Both make about the same number of faces, but the faces have different shapes. Marching Cubes cuts each cell by a table, and about one triangle in ten comes out as a thin sliver. Surface Nets lays its quads out in a regular grid, so its faces are more even and easier to smooth or edit.

The price is accuracy. An averaged point can sit a little off the true surface, and neither method keeps a sharp corner: both round it off. Dual Contouring is built to fix that.
