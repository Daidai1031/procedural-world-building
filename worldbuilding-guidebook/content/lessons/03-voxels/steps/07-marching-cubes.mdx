Last step, one cell told us where the surface crosses it and roughly what shape it takes. **Marching Cubes** does that for every cell in the grid, one after another, and the pieces of skin fit together edge to edge into one surface.

For each cell it needs three things. First, the case index of its corners. Second, the row of the table for that index, which lists how to join the dots (the highlighted line). Third, where each dot sits on its edge. Nobody works the pattern out again for every cell: the table is built once, so reading a cell is almost free.

Switch between the two views and change the shape. The cubes can only step in whole cells. The Marching Cubes surface slides each point along its edge, so slopes and overhangs come out smooth. Neighbouring cells always agree on how a shared face is cut, so the surface has no cracks.
