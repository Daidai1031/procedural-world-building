Behind this card is a slice through some ground. In the **height field** view, every position on the map stores exactly one number: how high the ground reaches, shown as a yellow dot. Below the dot is solid and above it is air, so a height field can never hold a cave. One height cannot say "solid, then empty, then solid again."

Switch to the voxel view. A **voxel** is a pixel with a third dimension: one sample in a regular grid along x, y, and z. Each sample stores its own **density** value, so the grid can hold a chamber, the hollow under an **overhang**, even blocks floating in the air. The pink dots mark empty samples with ground above them.

The sign of that value follows the negative-inside **signed distance field** (SDF) rule:

- `sdf(p) < 0` is inside the solid.
- `sdf(p) > 0` is air.
- `sdf(p) = 0` is the surface.

For the ground, the value is `y - height`. It is negative below the ground and positive above it.

Voxel data is not a picture yet. It only records inside and outside. The next steps turn that boundary into geometry.
