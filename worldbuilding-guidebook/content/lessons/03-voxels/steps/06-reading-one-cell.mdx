Voxel data is only numbers, so the surface has to be found between them. Marching Cubes looks at one **cell** at a time: the small cube between eight neighbouring samples. Each corner is inside (negative) or outside (positive), and the question is how the surface passes through the cell.

If all eight corners agree, the surface misses the cell. If they disagree, it must cross every edge with an inside end and an outside end. That is what the highlighted line keeps. Pick a pattern in the control panel and find the pink dots: they sit on exactly those edges. A dot is not always in the middle. It sits closer to the corner whose value is closer to zero.

Now switch to the surface view. Joining the dots that share a face gives flat pieces of skin, and every pattern gives a different shape.

The number in brackets is the pattern's **case index**, a label the next step uses to look up how to join the dots.
