Curved geometry is built from small flat faces. The geometry lines below show a
pattern: size arguments come first, followed by segment counts that divide the
surface more finely.

The sphere uses `sphereGeometry args={[1, 48, 48]}`. Its radius is 1; the two 48s
set segments around its width and along its height. The cone uses `cylinderGeometry
args={[0, 0.9, 1.8, 32]}`: top radius 0, bottom radius 0.9, height 1.8, then 32
segments around. A zero top radius turns this cylinder into a cone.

The knot's `torusKnotGeometry args={[0.6, 0.2, 160, 32]}` gives radius 0.6 and tube
radius 0.2, then 160 segments along the winding path and 32 around the tube.
More segments make the curves smoother, but also give the computer more faces
to draw. Segment counts change detail; the earlier arguments set the size.

Select the green knot and orbit slowly. Its highlight slides around the curved
surface, showing how the same material responds at many angles. On the box, each
flat face holds a single orientation.
