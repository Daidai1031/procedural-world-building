Select the sphere, then switch back to the box. Both use `meshStandardMaterial`,
shown below inside their meshes, but their surface values differ.

**Roughness** describes how uneven a surface is up close. The box uses 0.65, so its
brightness spreads across each face. The sphere uses 0.15, so reflections look
sharper. Compare the two `roughness` lines, then orbit to watch the highlights move.

**Metalness** changes how the surface reflects light. The box uses 0.05, close to a
non-metal. The sphere uses 0.9, close to a metal, so its appearance depends heavily
on the surroundings reflected in it. `SPHERE_BLUE` names its palette color, just as
`BOX_ORANGE` names the box's color. Both materials use `envMapIntensity={0.45}`;
the different finishes come from their roughness and metalness, not a different
reflection strength.

The sphere's `sphereGeometry` starts with radius 1. Its two remaining arguments
control how finely the surface is divided, which we will compare with the other
curved shapes shortly.
