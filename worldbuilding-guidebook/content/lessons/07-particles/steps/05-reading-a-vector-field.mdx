Wind blows differently in different places. To describe that, you do not write a rule for every speck. You draw a **wind map**: at every spot on the ground, one arrow. The arrow points where the wind goes, and a longer arrow means stronger wind. Mathematicians call this map a **vector field**.

A speck uses the map by looking at the arrow **under itself**, right now, and moving that way. Two specks in different places read different arrows, so they drift apart.

The arrows can mean two things. In a **velocity field** the arrow is the speed itself, like a river current carrying a leaf. In an **acceleration field** the arrow is a push that changes the speed, like a tilted floor under a ball. The picture looks the same, so the map must always say which kind it is.

Click the ground or press **Drop leaves**, then switch **Field** and **Arrow means** to compare. Notice that leaves can drift straight through the ground: the map moves them, but it does not know where the ground is.
