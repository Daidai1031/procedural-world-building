title: Trails and visual variation
goal: Make motion history and particle state visible without changing the movement rule.

A **trail** stores a particle's recent positions and draws lines between them. The particle shows where it is now; the trail shows where it has been. Capping the stored history keeps the trail's length and memory cost under control. When a particle is recycled, its history must be cleared, or a line jumps across to the spot where it respawns.

Appearance can also show state. Mapping speed to colour reveals fast and slow regions. Mapping normalised age, age divided by lifetime, to opacity can fade a particle out over its life. These mappings change what the motion communicates, not the field that causes it.

A small moving dot can be hard to read; a trail makes direction and curvature visible at a glance. Recording history at fixed simulation intervals keeps trails consistent across frame rates.
