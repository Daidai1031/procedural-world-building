A **uniform** field points the same way everywhere. A **vortex** turns around a chosen centre. A noise-driven **perturbation** varies direction or strength across space and makes the flow irregular. In this lesson, "turbulence" names that artistic perturbation, not a solved physical turbulence model.

Fields combine by adding their vectors at each position. Added to a uniform flow, a vortex bends nearby arrows into a local swirl while the overall drift continues. Combined vectors can strengthen, weaken, or cancel each other; opposing contributions do not automatically blend into a smooth, realistic transition.

For a flat vortex around `(cx, cz)`, an unnormalised direction turning around the centre is `(-(z - cz), 0, x - cx)`. Its strength needs a safe profile near the centre, so the field never divides by zero or grows out of control.

A three-dimensional field extends the same query into a volume. A fluid solver adds further physical rules and is a later exploration.
