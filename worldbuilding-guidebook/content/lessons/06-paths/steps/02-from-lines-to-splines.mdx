A **polyline** joins positions with straight segments. A **spline** describes a curve from **control points** and an interpolation rule. This lesson uses a **Catmull–Rom** curve, which passes through every point it is given. Other curve types, such as Bézier curves, use handles that the curve bends towards but does not pass through.

Moving one interior point shows the difference. The polyline changes only the two straight segments touching that point; the spline changes a curved region around it. More control points give more control over the shape, but they do not by themselves make a route fit the terrain.

Smoothing can also add unwanted bends or overshoot, especially near sharp turns. A smooth curve still needs constraints when it stands for a practical road.
