---
title: Paths and terrain influence each other
goal: Distinguish following a surface from altering the surface, and use terrain to inform a route.
keywords:
  - terrain deformation
  - influence band
  - road profile
  - route cost
  - blend weight
---

A projected path follows the terrain without changing it. A **road layer** can also reshape the ground around it: measure how far each terrain sample is from the road centerline, then blend its height towards a **road profile** inside an **influence band**.

The profile supplies the road's height along its length. A road can be flatter across its width while still climbing gently along the route. The **road width** sets the central region; the **shoulder width** sets how gradually the ground returns to its original shape. Widening either one changes more terrain.

The influence also runs the other way. Candidate routes can be compared by a **route cost**, such as steepness or total climb, and the cheaper one preferred. That is a route-selection rule, different from pressing an existing route into the surface.

For rivers, a lowered channel alone does not make water flow downhill. Elevation and flow rules matter too.
