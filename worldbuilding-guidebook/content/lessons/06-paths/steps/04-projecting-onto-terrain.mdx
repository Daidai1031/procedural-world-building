---
title: Projecting onto terrain
goal: Convert a path over a map into positions on a terrain surface.
keywords:
  - projection
  - height query
  - height field
  - raycasting
  - sample density
---

A path drawn in the x–z plane does not yet know the terrain's height. **Projecting** it means sampling the path, asking the terrain for the height at each sample's x and z, and using that as the sample's y. A small offset above the surface keeps the line visible instead of flickering into the ground.

The projected points follow hills and valleys, but the straight segments between them can still cut through a bump if the samples are too far apart. More samples make that error smaller.

A simple height lookup works here because this is a **height field**: every x–z position has exactly one surface height. A mesh with overhangs, or a spherical planet, needs a surface query that fits its geometry, such as casting a ray from a chosen origin in a chosen direction.

A new spline drawn through projected points can leave the surface again between them, so its samples have to be checked or projected once more.
