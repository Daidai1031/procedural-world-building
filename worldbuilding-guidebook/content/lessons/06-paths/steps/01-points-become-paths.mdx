---
title: Points become paths
goal: Understand a path as an ordered connection rather than an unconnected set of positions.
keywords:
  - path
  - order
  - connectivity

---

A **path** connects positions in a particular **order**. The same three points can form different routes when their order changes: A → B → C and A → C → B use exactly the same points, yet trace different lines and end in different places.

A line gains meaning through what it is used for. It might be a road centerline, a river course, a rail alignment, or a route for something that moves. On its own it has no width, no surface material, and no rules for junctions. Each of those has to be added by the system around it.

Giving the points a direction, from A to B to C, can describe a journey or a flow. Drawing an arrow, though, does not simulate vehicles or water; those behaviours need rules of their own.

A list of points becomes a path once the system uses that order to connect or travel along them.
