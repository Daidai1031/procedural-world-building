The result

Robots are told apart by their outline (ring, basket, roller), not by color, because in orbit each one is only about 28 pixels tall. The planet reads through contour lines and gray values, and the air reads as fine dust.

MinerE · Voxel orbCube ball, tilted ring, stepped drill. Graphite band.
GardenerG3 · Pannier workerSeed basket and two arms. Moss band.
BuilderB1 · RollerCube roller on two brackets. Ochre band.
Status lightOne light per robotWhite pulse = scanning, amber = moving, fast amber = working, gold = collecting, green = planting, rust = stopped.
PlantsA + DLichen patch with a branching plant that grows by level.

RenderingSolidNo outlines. One soft light and a warm fill.

How we chose

1 · Build every optionEach idea became a small 3D model at the real game scale, so sizes could be judged honestly.
2 · Compare side by sideOptions were shown together on one board, on the same planet and in the same light.
3 · Pick and give feedbackEach round ended with picks. Comments like “too round” or “the dashed line looks bad” became the next round.
4 · Apply to the gameOnly the look changed. Robot behavior and game rules stayed exactly as they were.

Scale rules used throughout: one voxel is 0.095 units; a robot must fit inside the tunnel it digs (about 0.2 wide); details smaller than 0.05 units disappear from the orbit camera.
