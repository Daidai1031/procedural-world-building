Two of the three lights here throw no shadow at all. They are not failed lights —
they are doing a job shadows would get in the way of.

**Ambient light** adds the same small amount of brightness, 0.4, to every surface in
the scene, no matter which way it faces. It is the reason the dark side of the
sphere is dark grey rather than black. Real rooms behave this way: light bounces off
walls and floors until it arrives from everywhere at once, and ambient light is the
cheap approximation of that.

**Hemisphere light** is directional in a soft way. It sends a cool blue down from
above and a warm brown up from below, and blends between them depending on how much
a surface tilts up or down. Look at the sphere's underside against its top and you
can see it: slightly warm below, slightly cool above.

Neither can produce a shadow, because neither comes from a single place.
