Select the sphere, then switch back to the box a few times. The same three lights
fall on both, yet they look nothing alike. Two numbers on their materials account
for almost all of that difference.

**Roughness** describes how uneven a surface is up close. The box is set to 0.65 —
rough, so light scatters off it in every direction and the brightness spreads evenly
across each face. The sphere is 0.15 — smooth, so light bounces in a consistent
direction and gathers into one tight, bright spot.

**Metalness** decides where the colour comes from. The box is 0.05, essentially
non-metal, so you see its own orange. The sphere is 0.9, near-metal, so most of what
you see is the scene reflected back at you, tinted blue.

Rough and non-metal reads as chalk, clay, or unfinished wood. Smooth and metallic
reads as chrome or polished steel.
