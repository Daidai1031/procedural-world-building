Select the yellow cone and orbit around it. Its sides never get brighter or darker,
and it has no highlight anywhere. The box and the sphere both shade as you move;
this one refuses to.

That is because it uses a different kind of material — one with no roughness and no
metalness, because it does no lighting calculation at all. Its colour is simply its
colour, drawn flat. Turn every light in the scene off and the cone would look
exactly as it does now.

It still casts a shadow onto the floor, though. Whether an object blocks light and
whether its own surface responds to light are two unrelated settings, and this cone
has the first without the second.

Flat materials are the cheapest thing you can draw, which makes them useful for
markers, outlines, and deliberately stylised worlds.
