The camera started at 6 units right, 5 up, and 8 forward, looking at a point just
above the centre of the floor. That point is the **target**, and it matters more
than it sounds: the camera always turns to face it, so the target is what you orbit
around rather than the camera itself.

**Field of view** is the width of the cone the camera can see, set here to 50
degrees. Widen it and more of the scene fits in, but edges stretch and near things
loom. Narrow it and the scene flattens out, the way a long lens flattens a face.
Fifty degrees is an unremarkable middle, which is exactly what you want for looking
at a few objects on a floor.

Two last numbers, **near** and **far**, set the range that gets drawn — 0.1 to 200
units from the camera. Anything nearer or further is skipped entirely, which is why
you cannot push the view inside a shape and watch it turn inside out.
