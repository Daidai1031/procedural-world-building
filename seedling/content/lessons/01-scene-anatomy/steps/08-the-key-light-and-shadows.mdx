The third light does the real work. It sits high and to one side, at 8 units right,
12 up, and 5 forward, and shines across the whole scene in parallel lines, like the
sun. At intensity 2.5 it is far stronger than the other two, which is why it decides
where every highlight and every shadow lands.

Shadows are not automatic. Four separate things have to agree, and if any one is
missing they vanish without an error:

1. The canvas has to be rendering shadows at all.
2. The light has to be marked as casting them — it renders the scene from its own
   point of view into a 2048 by 2048 image, and that image is what says which
   surfaces are hidden from it.
3. The object has to be marked as casting a shadow.
4. The surface underneath has to be marked as receiving one.

When a shadow you expected does not appear, walk that list in order. It is almost
always one of those four.
