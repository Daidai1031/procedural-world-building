Select the green torus knot at the back. It is not one of the simple shapes — it is
a tube wound around a path that loops through itself, built by tracing that curve
160 times around and 32 times across.

Orbit around it slowly and watch the bright spot on its surface. On the box, the
highlight sits still on a face and then jumps to the next one. On the torus knot it
slides continuously, brightening and fading as the surface turns towards the light
and away again.

That is the practical argument for curved geometry, and it is not about detail for
its own sake. A flat face only ever shows you the material at one angle. A curved
one shows you every angle at once, which is why a sphere or a knot is the honest
test of whether a material is doing what you think it is.
